## The brief

> Report on one lost or abandoned game: what happened to it, what evidence
> survives, and what that evidence supports.

This is not the recovery project — you are not expected to have a working
build by week 6. What you're building instead is the practice the final
project depends on: choosing a real case, sourcing it honestly, and stating
clearly what the evidence does and doesn't prove.

You may report on a game other than the one you'll recover for the final
project. Some students use the Field Report to test a candidate artifact
before committing to it in week 8; that's a reasonable use of it.

## What you submit

A short written report (the exact length and format are set by your tutor
at the start of the course) covering: what the game was, why it qualifies
as lost or abandoned under the course's definition, what evidence you found
and where it came from, and what that evidence lets you claim with
confidence versus what remains uncertain.
