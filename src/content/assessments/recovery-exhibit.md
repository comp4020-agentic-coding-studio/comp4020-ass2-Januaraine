## The brief

> Recover one lost or abandoned game, and show your work to people who
> weren't in the room for any of it.

This is the practicum's capstone: the recovery attempt itself, the report
that documents it, and the exhibition that makes it legible to someone
outside the course. All three are required — a strong recovery with no
report is unmarkable, and a report with no exhibition hasn't done the job
this brief actually asks for.

Recovering the artifact fully is not the requirement. A recovery that
stalls at a specific, well-evidenced obstacle — a corrupted archive, a
format nobody has documented, a dependency that no longer exists anywhere —
is a valid, completed piece of work if the report explains exactly why
recovery wasn't possible and what was tried.

## What you submit

Three parts, submitted together: whatever you were able to recover (a
build, an emulator profile, extracted assets — whatever your attempt
produced), a written archaeological report, and a presented exhibition in
the week 12 session per the [studio spec](/sessions/12-what-we-couldnt-recover/).
