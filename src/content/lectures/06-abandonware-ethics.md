"Abandonware" is not a legal category. A game with no current owner still
has a copyright somewhere, held by whoever inherited a publisher's assets —
often nobody who can be found, sometimes somebody who very much can. This
week lays out where that leaves you: what emulation is settled law, what
distributing a ROM or an old installer is not, and what "responsible
archival access" means in practice when you're relying on a preservation
community's upload rather than your own dumped hardware.

This course does not encourage distributing copyrighted material, and your
field report should never need to. The line it does ask you to hold: work
from what you can access responsibly, document where it came from, and be
honest in your report about the parts of the chain you can't verify.

- copyright doesn't expire just because a publisher did
