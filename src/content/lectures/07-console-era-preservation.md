Console preservation has its own vocabulary — cartridge dumping, BIOS
emulation, region locking, save-battery decay — and this week covers how
each of those works, without asking you to own a flash cart or a soldering
iron. Owning the hardware helps if you already have it, but it is not a
prerequisite for this course: existing dumps and preservation-community
archives are enough to work with a console-era artifact at the depth this
course asks for.

The point isn't to make everyone a hardware hobbyist. It's to understand
*why* a console dump is trustworthy evidence (or isn't) when you find one —
what a checksum tells you, what a bad dump usually looks like, and why a
battery-backed save can die years before the cartridge that holds it does.

## Outline

- what dumping a cartridge actually captures, in principle
- BIOS emulation and why it matters for accuracy
- judging whether a dump you didn't make yourself is trustworthy
- reading an archive's provenance notes like part of the evidence
