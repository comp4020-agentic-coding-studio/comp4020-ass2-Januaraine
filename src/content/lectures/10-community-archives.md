Most lost games have no institutional archive at all — what exists instead
is scattered across abandonware sites, preservation-community wikis, and old
forum threads where someone posted a beta build in 2009 and nobody has
touched the link since. This week treats those as primary sources with
their own evidentiary standards: who uploaded a file, what they claimed
about its provenance, and whether later posts in the same thread corroborate
or contradict the original claim.

This isn't a detour into games history for its own sake. It's a method: a
forum thread that pins down when a build was uploaded, or a wiki edit that
records a publisher's closure date, can be exactly the evidence a recovery
report needs and can't get anywhere else.

## Outline

- why community archives, not institutions, hold most of this evidence
- reading provenance claims in an upload or a forum post critically
- corroboration: when a second source changes what a claim is worth
- citing a forum thread properly in a recovery report
